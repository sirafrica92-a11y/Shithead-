# Owner source Part Two — original multi-player examples

**Provenance:** Africa supplied these examples after the master prompt on 16 October 2026. This is a **close, structured transcription** (not verbatim punctuation), preserving mechanics, roles and example outcomes. Part Two is more specific than Part One and controls conflicting interpretations.

## 3 and Joker are identical

- Same attacking and defending powers.
- Attack: send the **playing pile** to any chosen other player.
- Defence: targeted player holding a 3 or Joker can instead remove it and send the pile back to the attacker **or** to any other player. Counter chains can continue until a target accepts.
- A targeted player may choose to **accept anyway**, even if holding a counter, as a strategy.

## Two players — pile exists

- A 3/Joker sends the pile to the only other player.
- That player may redirect it back with their own 3/Joker.
- The two may counter back and forth as long as their cards allow.
- At any point the current recipient can choose to collect instead of countering.

## Two players — no pile

- If an actor only has 3/Jokers available, one may skip the opponent and let the same actor go again.

## Three players — pile exists

Seats A → B → C.

- A targets B. B lacks a counter and takes pile. B misses immediate turn; **C acts next**.
- A instead targets C. C takes pile, but **B acts next**; C does not lose their future turn just because they collected it.
- A can use two 3/Jokers: attack B then skip C to act again.
- Anyone targeted may counter to any player holding/not holding another 3/Joker; chains may return to earlier participants.

## Three players — no pile

- A spends a 3/Joker to skip B; C acts next.

## Four players — pile exists

Seats A → B → C → D.

- A has three 3s/Jokers and can attack B using first; B takes pile, then A can use others to skip C and D, letting A play again.
- An alternate illustration targets a non-next player. The source says "C or D", then works through **D picking up**: after additional skips of B and C, **D plays next**.
- With counters bouncing among players, if the final recipient is **B** (who would be next), B misses and C follows; otherwise normal next player **B** follows.

## Generalised logic for five and six

Africa affirmed the four-player pattern provides the general rule for 2–6; extrapolated A..E and A..F examples were created in subsequent discussion. They are **derived applications**, not further independent original scenario text.
